Keepsake Privacy Policy

Last updated 2 September 2026

Keepsake is a recipe app for households. It is currently in private beta, distributed through TestFlight to a small number of invited testers. This policy describes what the app collects, where that information goes, and how to have it removed.

Keepsake does not show ads, does not sell or share personal information, and does not track you across other apps or websites.

What Keepsake collects

Account Your email address, and a display name you choose. Email is used to sign you in and to identify you to the other members of your household. Signing in normally uses a code emailed to you; if you opt to set a password instead, it is held by our sign-in provider and is never stored by the app itself.
Your content Recipes you add, import or photograph, including ingredients, instructions, yields, notes you write while cooking, weekly meal plans, and grocery lists.
Photos Images you attach to a recipe, or photograph in order to import a recipe from a printed or handwritten page.
Diagnostics Crash and error reports when something goes wrong, so it can be fixed.
Usage A small, fixed list of anonymous events recording which features are used — for example that a recipe was saved or a weekly plan confirmed. An event may carry plain measurements alongside its name: a count, a duration, or a yes/no flag such as whether a cooking note was added. It never carries what you wrote. Search terms in particular are deliberately excluded — only the timing and the number of results are recorded.

Keepsake does not collect your location, contacts, health data, advertising identifiers, or the contents of your photo library beyond the specific images you choose to add.

Households, and who can see your content

Recipes and plans in Keepsake belong to a household, not to an individual. Every member of your household can see, edit, archive and delete everything in that household's library, including recipes you added and cooking notes you wrote. Only join a household with people you intend to share all of that with.

In this beta there is no way to leave a household once you have joined one. If you need to be removed, contact us using the address below and it will be done by hand.

Who else processes your information

Keepsake relies on a small number of service providers. Each receives only what it needs:

Supabase Hosts the database, file storage and sign-in. Your account, recipes, plans and photos are stored here.
Anthropic When you import a recipe from a web address or a photo, the page's text or the image is sent to Anthropic's Claude API to extract the recipe's structure. This happens on the server, and only for imports you start.
Sentry Receives error and crash reports. Recipe content, cooking notes and credentials are deliberately excluded, and web addresses in diagnostic traces have their query strings removed before sending.
PostHog Receives the anonymous feature-usage events described above.
Resend Delivers the sign-in code emails. Receives your email address.
Apple Distributes the beta through TestFlight, and tells us which builds are installed. Apple's own terms apply to that. See also the note below on sending a grocery list to Reminders.

These providers process data on our behalf. None of them are permitted to use it for their own purposes.

Importing from the web

When you import a recipe from a web address, Keepsake fetches that page from its own servers rather than from your device, and sends the page's text to Anthropic for extraction. The address you imported is stored with the resulting recipe so it can be credited and revisited.

Keepsake also keeps a separate record of the import itself, which retains that address as a history of what was imported and why it succeeded or failed. That record outlives the recipe: deleting a recipe forever removes the recipe, but not this import history.

Sending a grocery list to Reminders

If you choose to send a grocery list to Apple Reminders, Keepsake asks for permission and then writes the item text — which is derived from your recipes — into the reminders account you have set up on your device. Where that text is then stored is decided by that account rather than by Keepsake: for an iCloud account it goes to Apple, and for another provider's account it goes to that provider. This only happens when you ask for it.

How long it is kept, and how to delete it

Your content is kept for as long as your household exists. Deleting a recipe moves it to Recently Deleted, where it stays until you restore it or choose Delete Forever. Deleting it forever removes the recipe and deletes its stored images. Image deletion is best-effort: if it fails, the file can remain in storage. A periodic sweep clears leftover photos taken for import, and anything else can be removed on request.

This beta has no in-app button to delete your account. To have your account and your household's data deleted, email the address below and it will be carried out manually. An in-app path is planned before any public release.

Security

Traffic between the app and its servers is encrypted in transit. Access to household data is enforced by database-level row security, so one household cannot read another's, and the app never holds administrative database credentials.

No system is perfectly secure, and this is a beta. Please do not store information in Keepsake that would be harmful to disclose.

Children

Keepsake is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.

Changes

If this policy changes materially, the date at the top will be updated and testers will be told through the app or by email.

Contact

Questions, or a deletion request: privacy@brightbench.app
